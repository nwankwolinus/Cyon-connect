## 3. Existing domain and implementation baseline

The identity migration `supabase/migrations/20261008090000_identity_foundation.sql` defines `public.members` with `membership_status` values `pending`, `active`, `inactive`, `suspended`, and `archived`; it defines `public.user_accounts` with `account_status` values `pending_activation`, `active`, `suspended`, and `deactivated`. The two status columns are independent. `docs/architecture/DATABASE_IDENTITY.md` states a member may exist without an account, and that account creation/linking and status changes require attribution. It also states lifecycle audit is not implemented.

The existing domain types in `modules/identity/domain/contracts.ts` reproduce the status vocabularies but do not define transitions. The only identity application use case and ports found are the read-only Get My Identity flow (`modules/identity/application/get-my-identity.ts`, `modules/identity/application/ports.ts`). It requires an active account and active membership for normal identity-context retrieval. That policy is separate from membership review and account activation.

The repository also implements a separate prospective-applicant Auth flow in `modules/applicant-auth` and `app/auth`/`app/api/applicant-auth`: email/password registration, confirmation callback, recovery, and server-verified applicant access. `modules/applicant-auth/infrastructure/supabase-applicant-session.ts` calls Auth `getUser()` and requires confirmed email through the applicant verification use case. `/applicant` hosts the Stage 2 membership application form, which the Project Owner formally accepted and closed; its data remains in temporary client-side memory and is not submitted or persisted. No application persistence, review route, or lifecycle operation exists. The owner-reported hosted integration observations are summarized in [APPLICANT_AUTHENTICATION_INTEGRATION_TEST.md](../runbooks/APPLICANT_AUTHENTICATION_INTEGRATION_TEST.md); they are not automated results or independent database/security verification. Local code does not establish the hosted configuration or production email delivery.

The inspected schema and application modules do not contain a membership-application entity or lifecycle use case. `members` represents member identity and status, not a distinct application record. In particular, a resubmission linked to a prior rejected application and preservation of both application histories cannot be represented solely by changing `members.membership_status`. A separate application representation or another explicitly approved model will be needed; this specification does not approve a new table or schema. The existing `members_immutable` trigger rejects changes to `id` and `created_at` only; it does not make `org_unit_id` or `membership_status` immutable. The separate no-delete trigger prevents physical deletion. These database protections are distinct from the planning decision that application lifecycle operations must not mutate existing identity records.

**Owner-approved policy/design directions:** a submitted application is a separate durable record and does not require a member record. Create a new `public.members` identity only after approval; do not create one per application or resubmission. If an application may belong to an existing member, linking requires authorized review; similarity alone is not sufficient. Resolve a possible existing-member match before final membership approval; unresolved or insufficiently evidenced matches block approval and member creation. These decisions do not authorize merge, relink, or modification of an existing identity. A newly created post-approval member has status `active` as approved membership only; account activation remains separate.

## 6. Rejection, resubmission, and duplicates

Resubmission is permitted under rules to be defined. A resubmission must be a new application linked to its previously rejected application, preserving both histories. The current schema has no separate application record, so this relationship and retained history require implementation. `FR-MEM-002` states one member record per person. Before creating a new member after approval, the protected operation must perform the approved duplicate-review procedure; a foreign key or unique application/member link cannot establish that two submissions concern different people. Similarity alone never proves identity.

Potential duplicate detection must not automatically create another application or reject an applicant. It routes the case for authorized review. Duplicate review requires explicit permission and defined scope. Routine Secretary authority is not unlimited authority over identity conflicts; escalated or disputed cases follow the owner-approved President authority boundary.

The separate open decision concerns duplicate-member merging and identity repair. This contract covers screening and resolution needed for the application flow, but does not authorize merging, transfers, or identity repair.

**Owner-approved policy:** resolve a potential existing-member match through authorized review before final membership approval. No conflict was found in `FR-MEM-004` (application review) or `FR-MEM-011` (route potential duplicates for authorized review); neither independently specifies sequencing. The Project Owner approved this policy on 2026-10-22. The current schema has no application or match-review representation; implementation must define it, permitted evidence, permissions and scope. Until resolution, fail closed and do not finalize approval or create/associate a member.

**Approved conceptual screening contract:** screening may record (1) no apparent match, which is not proof of uniqueness; (2) possible match; (3) confirmed match; or (4) insufficient evidence. Do not invent a matching algorithm, threshold, unique personal identifier, or external verification. A possible match or insufficient evidence requires authorized human review with explicit action permission and parish scope. Record reviewer, scope, disposition, concise rationale/evidence reference, and time, minimizing PII. The review operation and permission assignment are not implemented; until they exist, deny the operation. A confirmed match may be associated only after that authorized determination, without creating a member or changing existing identity/account links. “No apparent match” permits consideration of new-member creation only after the approved screening procedure is recorded; database constraints cannot prove person-level uniqueness.

**Unresolved:** approved screening data/evidence, reviewer permissions and scope, dispositions, and the relationship between a possible match and an existing member. No automatic merge or identity mutation is authorized here.

Application/member association cardinality, preservation of historical associations, and correction versus supersession are also unresolved. An association correction must be separately authorized and attributable; it must not rewrite the application lineage or mutate existing member/account identity links.

**FR-MEM-002 / FR-SEC-003 creation gate:** the protected operation must require and transactionally revalidate a completed duplicate-screening/review result. Possible-match or insufficient-evidence outcomes block final approval, association and creation under the owner-approved sequencing rule; “no apparent match” is not a database guarantee of uniqueness. A foreign key or unique application/member link proves record relationships, not person uniqueness. Before enabling the path, define privacy-reviewed evidence, review representation, permission and scope enforcement.

## 7. Account activation following membership approval

`FR-MEM-005` says approved members enter a defined account-activation workflow. The project-owner decision is that approval starts that separate workflow and never activates an account automatically. Membership and account statuses remain independent.

The applicant completes a secure verification/confirmation flow. A controlled system workflow activates an account only after required identity and security checks pass. The Secretary and President do not manually grant account activation as a side effect of reviewing an application.

No account transition table is specified. The following remain unresolved: source/target account states; verification factor and identity checks; user confirmation; token/challenge expiry; retry limits; idempotency and replay rules; concurrency; error/recovery behavior; and attribution/audit representation for automated activation. Failed or expired verification must not activate an account or bypass checks, but the persisted failure state is not defined here.

Membership status is independent. The migration defines `pending`, `active`, `inactive`, `suspended`, and `archived`, with a database default of `pending`; it defines no transition semantics. `FR-MEM-009` defines vocabulary, not transitions. **Owner-approved policy:** create a new member as `active` in the same transaction as final approval, with `active` meaning approved membership only. Keep account creation/activation separate under `FR-MEM-005`; every access path must enforce required account activation, and membership status alone must not grant access. Do not use the `pending` database default to override this decision. This lifecycle operation is not implemented.

For a confirmed existing association: an `active` member may be associated without a status change. For `pending`, `inactive`, `suspended`, or `archived`, do not change, reactivate or restore the member; hold completion where approval depends on a status change for a separately approved disposition. Never change account status or alter `user_accounts.member_id` in this flow. This no-implicit-mutation boundary is approved; separate status transitions remain outside this contract.

**Approved approval-to-member design contract (not implemented):** final approval requires an authenticated actor with the approved action permission, application scope, and current application state; a completed and transactionally revalidated duplicate-screening disposition; and either a confirmed existing-member association with an allowed status disposition or authorization to create a new member. Create a new member only in the same protected operation that commits approval, with status `active` as approved membership only. Keep account activation separate. Commit the application outcome, member creation/association, business review-history event, and required audit event in one PostgreSQL transaction. If any validation, concurrency/version check, constraint, member operation, or required audit write fails, roll back all those database changes and report no completed approval. A retry uses the same operation identity and reconciles to the prior result rather than create a second member. External notifications remain outside the transaction. This design direction is owner-approved; implementation and tests are incomplete.

## 8. Authorization and organizational scope

Every protected review, decision, duplicate-review, activation, and audit-read action must be enforced server-side. The authorization boundary must establish the authenticated actor, check an explicit permission, enforce applicable parish/organizational scope, and validate the current application state before changing business state. Client-side UI restrictions are not authorization.

No permission identifiers, role-to-permission assignments, or final scope model are created here. The role catalog and executive permissions remain subject to their own approval and design. A `PLATFORM_ADMIN` role alone does not grant membership-review, approval, activation, duplicate-resolution, or unrestricted audit access.

## 9. Audit events, transactional consistency, and denied attempts

The owner-approved minimum audit fields are:

- Authenticated actor identifier, or a clearly defined system-actor identity for automated actions.
- Target member or application identifier.
- Action.
- Previous and resulting state, where applicable.
- Timestamp.
- Outcome and failure category, where applicable.
- Correlation or evidence reference, where available.

Security-relevant denied attempts must be recorded without secrets or unnecessary sensitive payloads through a separately reliable mechanism where the business transaction cannot persist them. A business-state change must not commit unless its required audit event succeeds in the same transaction.

A denied request may not have a business transaction to share, and a denial discovered inside a transaction that rolls back cannot rely on a row written only within that failed transaction. A separately reliable denied-event path is required, but its mechanism, delivery guarantees, and behavior when that path is unavailable remain open. This specification does not claim denied attempts are currently captured reliably.

Ordinary application roles must not modify or delete audit history. Protection must exist at database and server-side boundaries, not solely in the UI. Audit reads require explicit authorization and scope. Apply data minimization and mask sensitive fields.

## 10. Audit access, retention, and governance

Audit access is role-based, read-only, and limited by explicit permission and defined scope. A platform administrator does not automatically receive unrestricted audit-log or membership-data access.

Before production implementation, the appropriate authority must approve a retention schedule. The following are unresolved: retention owner and period; access matrix for membership versus security events; parish versus cross-parish scope; masking rules; incident review/escalation ownership; disposal and legal-hold handling; applicable privacy/legal requirements; denied-event reliability; and audit export/review procedures.

## 13. Acceptance criteria for a future implementation

When the unresolved decisions applicable to a selected increment are approved, its implementation can be accepted when:

1. The Secretary can perform only approved routine review actions and the President only the approved oversight/escalation actions; unauthorized actors and out-of-scope targets are denied server-side.
2. Review operations validate the current application state at the protected mutation boundary and reject stale or conflicting decisions without partial business-state changes.
3. Approval initiates the separately defined activation workflow and does not itself activate the account.
4. Rejection remains distinct from membership status; resubmission preserves the prior application's history and link, according to the approved application model.
5. Potential duplicates are routed for authorized review without automatic application creation, rejection, member merging, or identity relinking.
6. No member identity is created for an application before approval; any association to an existing member requires authorized review and cannot alter that member.
7. Account activation occurs only through the approved controlled system workflow after its checks pass; expired, invalid, replayed, or failed verification cannot activate an account.
8. Required audit evidence for a committed state change is committed atomically with that change; denied-attempt capture is tested against its separately approved reliability mechanism.
9. Audit mutation/deletion is denied to ordinary application roles, and audit reads enforce the approved permission, scope, and masking policy.
10. Tests cover permitted and unauthorized review, invalid/stale states, activation separation, resubmission/history, duplicate routing, audit failure/rollback, denied-event failure behavior, replay, and scope boundaries as defined by approved decisions.

These criteria implement the owner-approved policy baseline while leaving the listed technical details open; they do not prescribe unknown status transitions or state names.
